📚 Grammar Reference Guide

Present Simple

Use: To talk about general facts, routines, and what normally happens in a season or place.

Structure:

(+) It + Verb(s)
(-) It + doesn't + Verb
(?) Does + it + Verb?

Time Markers: usually, normally, every day, in winter, often.

"It usually rains heavily in Nayarit during July."

Present Progressive

Use: To talk about actions happening right now, at this exact moment. It is a temporary action.

Structure:

(+) It + is + Verb(ing)
(-) It + isn't + Verb(ing)
(?) Is + it + Verb(ing)?

Time Markers: now, right now, at the moment, look!, listen!

"Look! It is raining right now. Grab an umbrella."

Stative Verbs (Feelings & States)

Some verbs express a state, feeling, or possession rather than a physical action. Even if you are feeling it "right now", we usually do NOT use the -ing form. We use the Present Simple.

Thoughts

  • Know
  • Believe
  • Understand

Feelings

  • Love / Hate
  • Want / Need
  • Prefer

Senses & States

  • Feel
  • Seem
  • Cost

Incorrect:

"I am wanting a hot coffee."

Correct:

"I want a hot coffee."
